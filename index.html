FAQ

The reasoning behind how this is built, not just what it does — this is written for whoever on your team is deciding whether to trust it.

Why does cancelling a booking need a client email click, not just a name/email match?

Earlier versions checked that the agent's stated clientEmail/clientPhone matched the record on the booking. That stops a wrong guess, but it doesn't prove the person asking actually is the client — an API key authorizes the agent to manage a business's bookings, not to vouch for who's messaging it on any given day. Someone who knows (or guesses) a client's email could still ask the agent to cancel their appointment.

So cancelling now queues the request and emails a real confirm link to the address on file. Clicking it is what a typed claim can't be: proof the requester actually has access to that inbox. The booking stays fully active — untouched — until they do.

Why doesn't a free booking confirm immediately the way a paid one does?

A paid booking already has a natural confirmation gate built in: the client has to open a real payment link and pay before anything is final. A free (collectPayment: false) booking had no equivalent — it used to confirm on the agent's word alone, which meant a stranger's name and email could land on the calendar as a real appointment with zero involvement from the actual client.

The fix mirrors cancellation: a free booking now holds the slot briefly and emails the client a link. Nothing is a real appointment — nothing shows up as booked anywhere — until they click it themselves.

Why hold the slot for a short window instead of not holding it, or holding it forever?

Not holding it at all means someone else could grab the exact same time in the minute it takes a client to open an email — the whole point of checking availability first would be undermined. Holding it indefinitely means an appointment nobody ever actually confirmed could squat on a provider's calendar forever, quietly costing them real bookings.

A short, clearly-stated window (currently 20 minutes, always returned as holdMinutes in the API response) is the middle path: real protection against a double-booking race, with an honest expiry if nobody follows through.

Why is there no refund endpoint?

Scheduling actions — holding a slot, creating a booking, cancelling one — are cheap to get wrong and cheap to reverse. Moving money isn't. A refund issued by an agent that misread a conversation is a real financial mistake, not an inconvenience to smooth over. That asymmetry is why refunds are the one thing deliberately left out of the API entirely — always a human, always from the business's own dashboard.

Why can a receptionist (human or AI) see every booking on the team, but not pricing or payouts?

It's the same access you'd hand a front-desk hire on day one: enough context to run the calendar for the whole team, zero visibility into the business's financial account. The agent gets exactly that — not more because it's software, not less because it's useful.

If a business has a team, why doesn't one team member's booking block another's calendar?

Because they're different people. Early on, availability checks were scoped to the whole business account rather than to the specific person being booked — which meant one barber's 2pm appointment made every other barber (and the owner) look busy at 2pm too, even though nobody else's calendar had actually changed. That's fixed: availability, holds, and conflict checks are all scoped per-provider now, so a team member's schedule only ever competes with their own bookings.
